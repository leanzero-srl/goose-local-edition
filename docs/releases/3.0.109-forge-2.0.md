# 3.0.109 — Forge 2.0 (Scope Ledger 2)

## What happened
Forge 1.0 stopped separating the strongest models: its top five sit between 0.945 and 0.977, and a review on
9 October 2026 found scoring faults that make some published scores wrong (evals/swarm-bench/forge/DESIGN.md
§17.8). Forge 1.0 is frozen on leanzero.net; its scores stay as published.

## What is new
The Benchmark view's Forge family now runs **Forge 2.0**. One model, in goose, upgrades a working Forge app
(Scope Ledger, the Forge 1.0 reference app) to Scope Ledger 2 inside a budget of 300 model calls:

- a live migration of the stored ledger while events keep flowing;
- rate limits: a points quota per virtual hour shared with the people using the site, short burst limits,
  per-issue write limits, and a stretch where the quota is used up and the app has to wait;
- Forge's invocation time limits, consumer deliveries that run at the same time, and storage queries that lag
  behind recent writes;
- changes to the Jira site mid-run (a sprint closes, a board switches its estimation field, an issue moves to
  another board's sprint, an issue is deleted, a permission is revoked);
- an admin panel in UI Kit with server-side permission checks, a signed web trigger for CI, a custom field,
  hardened Forge LLM use, and a size and round-trip budget for how the two Custom UI surfaces start.

The score is `tests earned × critical defects × reliability`, then the caps. Reliability is new: the tests are in
18 groups, and each group multiplies the score by `1 − 0.10 × the shortfall of its worst test`, so a group whose
worst test fails completely takes 10% off the whole score, on top of the failed test's own weight. Together the
groups never take the score below a quarter of what the tests earned. The task text states the rule.

A run is scored on three seeded copies of the Jira site: each test keeps its worst result of the three, and the
excellence measurements keep their average.

A result carries pictures of the surfaces the app rendered (the dashboard widget and the sprint action in light
and dark and, when the admin page rendered, the admin panel drawn by the benchmark from the app's component tree,
not by Jira) and one recording of the graded browser session.

## How it was checked
- Our reference app scores 1.0000 on each of five seeded sites scored alone, and when three of them are scored
  together the way a run is.
- The untouched starting app (what the model is handed) scores 0.13.
- Ten deliberately broken copies of the reference app each lose the tests they should, with no unexplained loss.
- A second app, written independently from the task text and never run against a scoring site, scored 0.7467
  (tests earned 0.9696). We judged its losses to be its own defects: the reference app passes every test it lost.
- Before any published run, two trial runs were made, and every point they lost was audited against the task text
  by an independent reader and then by a second reviewer who tried to overturn the verdict. They judged seven
  tests unfairly graded, from six causes. Five causes were fixed before the scorer was frozen. We kept the sixth:
  a sprint that closes while the app is held back by the quota must still show its numbers as at the close. The
  task text states it and the reference app passes it.

## What does not change
Gauntlet 7.1 / 7.2 and the frozen Forge 1.0 are untouched: their scorers, probes, specs, fixtures and thresholds
are byte-identical, and their call budget stays 150. Their file lists were re-pinned only for the shared harness
files that gained the Forge 2.0 tier: bench_budget.py, isolated_tiers.py and run_build.py in all three, plus
release_manifest.py and test_bench_budget.py in the two Gauntlet lists. Forge 1.0 sessions stay in the app as
history; new Forge runs are Forge 2.0.
