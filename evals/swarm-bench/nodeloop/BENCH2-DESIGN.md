# BENCH2 — the sb-4 hardening design (workflow wf_7ccffd22-8f7, 2026-08-12)

Commissioned by Mihai at the 0.996 ceiling: make scoring HARDER and MORE GRANULAR — an
improvement to the benchmarking itself. 8 agents: 4 ground-truth readers, 3 competing designs
(deepen / harden / rescale), 1 adversarial judge that source-verified its claims before ranking.

## The four live bench bugs the judge verified in source (fix FIRST — rank 1-2)
- probes/vendor_trace.py:16 EXPECTED_TOTAL=47 vs the mock's 247 (stale docs example) — any
  dormant-probe wiring before this fix is wrong on arrival.
- vendor_service.py:141 _etag_for hashes only offset+limit — the mock's ETags LIE after any
  mutation; correct clients would 304 against changed data.
- score_build.py:558 _trace_split gives sync2 everything to end-of-trace (unbounded window).
- vendor_service.py:331 mark_phase reset heuristic `not name.endswith('2')` re-arms traps for
  a hypothetical sync3.

## Judge verdicts
- **A — DEEPEN EXISTING CHECKS** — determinism 8/10, discrimination 9/10, cost 6/10: ADOPT AS BACKBONE. The deterministic stage attacks exactly where the corpus says saturation lives (the ~26 saturated checks, the dormant create_payment/409 surface, all-binary Tier C behind the 11-check collinear cluster), every check traces to a spec sentence, and its run-twice determinism control is the single best idea in any design. Deductions: store_schema_truth's mode=ro open contradicts its own WAL rationale and can false-flag correct WAL-mode builds (fixable: copy db+wal+shm, open the copy); resync_cheapness's optimum=3 is asserted where C argues 1 — must be settled by what vendor_docs licenses, not by measurement alone; two input_validation_matrix cells are spec inferences (self-mitigated by the known-good adjudication gate); the browser stage is real flake risk but correctly exiled to sb-5 behind a gate that can only block the grader, never mislabel the model. Keeps opus-5-r0 as known-good with a measured re-baseline instead of demanding a hand-written trusted reference — the safer trust structure.
- **B — NEW HARDER REQUIREMENT CLASSES (Tier E)** — determinism 4/10, discrimination 7/10, cost 3/10: QUARRY, DO NOT ADOPT. Three fatal attacks: (1) concurrent_load_safe's 3s-timeout-vs-6s-stall and the wall-clock scale budget are load-sensitive on sweep hosts that run LLM inference — B's own risk list admits the FLAKE control cannot cover sweep-time load, and one flaky responsiveness failure poisons the repair loop, the CRITICAL axis; (2) it amends spec and vendor_docs in the same bump that hardens the grader, so no score movement across the boundary is attributable (harder task × stricter grader × 46-point spread); (3) the mandatory hand-written reference is a single point of trust with the exact shape of the stale-trace-field incident. It also leaves most of the 26 measured-saturated checks saturated — it opens new space rather than resolving the space the corpus proved dead. Take from it: update_propagation's 25-row granular fraction (better than A's single-row version), the event-keyed restart/kill mechanics, fixtures.py consolidation, and the strictest absence-guard discipline of the three designs.
- **C — RESCALING AND ROTATION** — determinism 8/10, discrimination 8/10, cost 4/10: ADOPT THE SKELETON, DEFER THE ROTATION. The core/hard split is the only mechanism in any design that makes 0.996-by-saturation structurally impossible rather than merely harder; the second_sync_cost merge implements the board's ONLY CONFIRMED adjudication (the 16.3% double-counted duplicate); excellent/solid run-rate is the correct instrument for a bench whose same-cell spread (0.65) dwarfs every check weight; the frozen-sb-3 dual-scoring transition is the cleanest comparability story offered. Rejected pieces: ks1 rotation (costs ~the whole original bench, C self-rates difficulty-matching confidence low, halves n against the 46-point spread, and memorization is not the measured problem — saturation is); promoting the two board-REFUTED artefact-prone checks (vendor_retry_date, vendor_cursor_expiry) into the hard block injects masked-non-observation zeros into the excellence flag; readme_run_verified's extraction misses inline-backtick commands and would zero compliant READMEs; the claim that relocating request_efficiency 'does the work' is half-false — its weight actually drops from 0.02 to 0.01; the cap structure, not the relocation, does the work.

## Synthesis

One grader-only version bump, sb-3 → sb-4, built as: Design A's deterministic deepening as the check backbone, mounted on Design C's aggregation skeleton (core/hard split + excellent-run-rate + merged conditional-pair), with Design B quarried for its two best checks (granular update_propagation, restart mechanics) and its absence-guard discipline. Everything timing-sensitive, every spec/docs amendment, the second spec, and the browser stage are rejected or deferred — the repair loop that consumes these findings cannot tolerate one flaky check, so the admission bar is 'provably deterministic on a loaded host', not 'passed the FLAKE control once'. Verified against source before judging: _etag_for hashes only offset+limit (vendor_service.py:141), _trace_split gives sync2 everything to end-of-trace (score_build.py:558), mark_phase's reset heuristic `not name.endswith('2')` would re-arm traps for 'sync3' (vendor_service.py:331), and the EXPECTED_TOTAL 247-vs-47 collision is live (score_build.py:45 vs probes/vendor_trace.py:16) — so the three infrastructure fixes all designs independently demand are confirmed prerequisites, not speculation. The A-vs-C dispute on the second-sync optimum (3 vs 1 requests) is resolved by the traceability rule, not measurement: once generation enters the ETag, one 304 does prove the collection unchanged, but nothing in vendor_docs licenses a client to rely on that, so charging a 3-conditional-GET client below 1.0 is a preference dressed as a defect — optimum_sync2 = 3 (all pages conditional at max limit), revisable only in a future docs-revision bump. Ceiling math after synthesis: a current 0.996-class build lands ≈0.90-0.93 (core near-saturated, hard block ≈0.4-0.6 on never-probed surfaces: 409-replay, update propagation, restart, instant-order traps), and 1.0 requires simultaneous optimum-request sync1, 3×304 sync2, replay-safe create, restart survival, and trap-pair-correct ordering — reachable in principle (honest, unlike the docstring's current claim) but by no existing build. Confidence flags per the standing rule: HIGH on ranks 1-8 (pure inspection/arithmetic over evidence already gathered or gathered deterministically); MEDIUM on rank 7's second-boot flake surface (mitigated by retry-once + sqlite cross-check via file copy, and by the controls' 5× known-good repetition); MEDIUM on rank 9's threshold choices (mitigated by calibrating on archive rescoring before freezing); the two adjudication-gated matrix cells are the only place a correct app could still be wrongly penalised, and the gate's drop-don't-rebase rule bounds that risk.

### #6 — Tier C graded rungs: convert cursor_paging, all_pages, cursor_expiry, conditional from binary to positive-evidence rungs; the two Retry-After checks stay binary
**From:** A (vendor_contract_graded)
**Why here:** Directly de-cliffs the corpus's documented worst structural failure — the 11-check byte-identical collinear cluster that carried 34% of all loss from one event — by turning C-tier cliffs into slopes, and finally honours the module's own first design law ('graded, not binary') which all seven C checks currently violate. Every rung requires an observed positive act — no rung reachable by absence.

### #7 — restart_persistence + sqlite ground truth: SIGKILL, reboot on the same --db, verify data/last_sync/page survival; direct sqlite inspection replacing store_indexed's regex

### #9 — Aggregation restructure: total = 0.90 × core + 0.10 × hard-block, plus excellent/solid run-rate as first-class per-arm k/n readouts
**From:** C (the split and the rate axis) — supersedes B's tier-E reweighting; hard block populated by the checks built in ranks 3-8
**Why here:** The only mechanism that makes 0.996-by-saturation structurally impossible: a core-perfect build caps at 0.90 and the last 0.10 is earnable only from request_efficiency, second_sync_cost's top rungs, 409-replay, update_propagation, restart, and the trap-pair ordering — all never-passed-clean surfaces. The rate axis is the honest instrument for a bench whose same-cell spread (0.65, standing 46-point calibration) exceeds every check weight. Sequenced after the checks exist because the split is arithmetic over them; MEDIUM confidence on threshold values, mitigated by calibrating on the archive before freezing.
**Implementation:** evaluate(): core = tier-weighted mean over A/B/C/hygiene (old-D regex greps) renormalized into 0.90; hard = plain mean of {request_efficiency (formula unchanged, optimum still the measured 7), second_sync_cost, create-replay pair, update_propagation, restart_persistence, trap-pair ordering, float-parts of row_integrity, concurrent_sync_consistent (C's parts upgrade: count/ids-distinct/both-shapes/no-5xx — post-hoc inspections only, no timing)}. Do NOT promote vendor_retry_date/vendor_cursor_expiry (board-REFUTED artefact history; masked zeros would noise the excellence flag). excellent := core≥0.98 AND hard≥0.50, solid := core≥0.90 — component thresholds so core failures can't be traded against hard wins; pinned only after archive rescoring calibration, any later move is a version bump. Sweep reports print k/n per arm, n always, never pooled across engine_build or scorer_version. Docstring amended to C's honest form: '100 is reachable only by an optimal client; a swarm build reaching ≥0.98 core triggers the next deepening.'

### #10 — Controls hardening — the gate that makes everything above believable: run-twice determinism control, per-new-check isolation defects, HIGH re-baselined to a measured sb-4 reference vector
**From:** A (determinism control, HIGH re-baseline, adjudication procedure) + B (defect menu and absence-guard review) + C (per-check defect list)
**Why here:** Ranked last in build order because it validates the finished set, but it is BLOCKING for rollout: no sb-4 verdict is believed before GRADER TRUSTED prints under the widened gate. The run-twice control operationalizes the no-flake law as something that REFUSES rather than something remembered (gates, not memory).
**Implementation:** controls.py: (1) score known-good twice per controls run — any per-check vector difference ⇒ GRADER NOT TRUSTED; run the full known-good 5× before first rollout with special attention to rank 7's second boot. (2) New isolation defects, each must move ONLY its expected set: insert_or_ignore (→update_propagation + store_atomic_upsert to 0.5), float_amounts (→row_integrity int part), in_memory_store (→restart+sqlite checks), client_signature_break (→client_probe_ran + attributed six), html_error_pages (→json_everywhere + matrix body cells), strip_if_none_match (→second_sync_cost + nothing else — proves the rank-3 merge killed the double-count), string_sort (→trap-pair check + chronological_order_full as a DECLARED expected pair). (3) HIGH becomes 'within ±0.03 of the sb-4 known-good reference vector measured 3× at rollout' — sb-4 is designed to pull opus-5-r0 below the stale 0.85 absolute, and keeping opus-5-r0 (a real build) as known-good avoids B's hand-written-reference single point of trust; a small reference CLIENT script is written only to measure/verify optima (sync1=7 unchanged, sync2=3), recorded with the measuring command per the existing optimum=7 discipline. (4) Any new matrix cell known-good fails goes to adjudication; contested cells are dropped, not rebased.

## Transition plan

Single grader-only bump: SCORER_VERSION 'sb-3' → 'sb-4', spec-build.md and vendor_docs.md byte-untouched — this is the deliberate rejection of Design B's coupled task+grader change, and it preserves the campaign's one continuous difficulty axis (any cross-boundary score movement is grader strictness + noise, with no task-change confound). Mechanics, in order: (0) FREEZE — copy the current scorer verbatim to bench/legacy/score_build_sb3.py with its own gather (C's step 0; an in-tree frozen copy, not just a git tag, because the sb-3 leg must run inside the same sweep process and sb-4's gather changes the trace sb-3's checks would read). (1) BRIDGE before belief — score three archived trees (known-good opus-5-r0, one mid-scoring cell, one broken cell) under both legs and publish the mapping, so '0.996 under sb-3' has a stated sb-4 equivalent (~0.90-0.93 expected, MEASURED not asserted) and the record never reads as a regression; the bridge also confirms known-good still walks the trap chain under the generation-aware ETag before anything is trusted. (2) GATE — sb-4 controls must print GRADER TRUSTED under the widened gate (run-twice determinism ×5, all old + new isolation defects, re-baselined HIGH) before any verdict is believed. (3) DUAL-SCORE WINDOW — for the next 2 engine_builds every cell runs the frozen sb-3 leg first (own boot), then sb-4; verdicts carry both numbers with version tags on the same report line; a voided leg records null; the two legs are never averaged and never pooled — the never-pool rule (§3.2) extends verbatim from engine_build to scorer_version. (4) BOARD — DEFECT-BOARD opens a new sb-4 corpus section; all sb-3 loss-carrier rankings freeze as historical (per-check weights themselves change, so cross-version per-check loss comparison is meaningless by construction); the sb-3 axis remains the longitudinal 'is the swarm improving' series until sb-4 accumulates n≥5 per cell against the standing 46-point spread. (5) CALIBRATE-THEN-PIN — archive rescoring calibrates the excellent/solid thresholds before they freeze; any later threshold, weight, fixture, or optimum change is sb-4.1+ (plan for one within two engine_builds rather than pretending the first pin holds). (6) sb-5 RESERVED — the browser stage ships only if the known-good tree produces byte-identical vectors on 5 consecutive control runs on a LOADED host; until then ui_states/ui_currency stay regex and the report annotates them as saturated — a declared limitation beats a flaky assertion. The additive-no-bump precedents (parts, attribution) continue to apply only to changes that move no number; nothing in this plan qualifies.

- TIER D (10 checks, weight 0.20, 0.02 per check): request_efficiency — granular ratio optimum/actual with optimum=7 (MEASURED: 3 pages at limit=100 + one 429 retry + the 410 restart + the HTTP-date retry; comment: 'Not a guess, and not 1 — the trap chain is unavoidable, so charging a client for it would be a deduction no implementation could escape'); uses_max_limit — granular max_limit_used/100; resync_conditional_ratio — granular sync2_304/sync2_reqs; concurrent_sync_safe — BINARY (total after two overlapping threaded syncs == 247); api_content_type — BINARY ('application/json' in Content-Type); ui_polish — granular fifths, 5 regexes on HTML (last-sync shown, disabled-while-syncing, row count, retry affordance, <th|scope= accessible table); store_atomic_upsert — STEPPED 1.0 (ON CONFLICT…DO UPDATE) / 0.5 (INSERT OR REPLACE/IGNORE, REPLACE INTO) / 0.3 (select-then-insert) / 0.0, regex SCOPED to the payments INSERT statement because a whole-file scan once matched an unrelated ON CONFLICT in the last_sync/meta write and passed a build whose sync path had lost its merge clause; store_indexed — BINARY regex (PRIMARY KEY|UNIQUE|CREATE INDEX in store.py); client_timeouts — BINARY regex (timeout= in meridian.py); ui_error_actionable — STEPPED 1.0 (try again|retry|check your connection|is the server running|refresh) / 0.4 (generic error|failed) / 0.0.
- BINARY vs GRANULAR summary: strictly binary (score can only be 0 or 1): server_runs, serves_page, total_field, ui_offline, all 7 C-tier checks, concurrent_sync_safe, api_content_type, store_indexed, client_timeouts — 13 checks. Stepped (2–3 fixed intermediate rungs): resync_idempotent (0.5), ui_currency (0.5), store_atomic_upsert (0.5/0.3), ui_error_actionable (0.4) — 4 checks. Truly granular fractions/ratios: modules_present, interfaces_declared, health_shape, sync_shape, sync_completeness, local_pagination, payment_row_shape, chronological_order, summary_accuracy, summary_bounds_utc, input_validation, ui_states, request_efficiency, uses_max_limit, resync_conditional_ratio, ui_polish — 18 checks. Despite the docstring's 'graded, not binary' rule, all of Tier C is binary.
- WEIGHTING: total = sum over tiers of (unweighted mean of that tier's check scores) × tier weight, rounded to 4 decimals (evaluate(), score_build.py:650-668). Checks are equally weighted WITHIN a tier, so one check's full weight in the total is: A = 0.25/6 ≈ 0.04167, B = 0.30/12 = 0.025, C = 0.25/7 ≈ 0.03571, D = 0.20/10 = 0.02.
- 0.996 DECOMPOSITION: 0.996 = 1.0 − 0.004 weighted deficit. The cheapest possible FULL miss is a D check at 0.02, so a 0.996 total cannot contain any fully-failed check — it must be partial credit on granular/stepped checks. Exact single-check constructions of 0.9960: any D-tier granular check at 0.80, e.g. ui_polish 4/5 regex hits, uses_max_limit largest limit 80, or resync_conditional_ratio 4/5 requests answered 304. Near misses for comparison: request_efficiency at 8 requests (7/8=0.875) gives 0.9975; at 9 requests gives 0.9956; chronological_order 23/24 ordered pairs gives 0.9990. Per the module docstring, a 0.996 means the instrument has nearly stopped measuring — 'if something ever does reach 100, that is the signal to deepen the task'.
- DELIBERATE KEEP #1 — SCORER_VERSION discipline (score_build.py:51-54): bump on ANY change to a check, weight, or fixture; a verdict carrying a different version is not comparable and the sweep re-runs rather than reuses it. Rationale in comment: without it a stale verdict scored by an older, buggier grader sat silently next to fresh ones — 'which is how a cheaper model appeared to beat a stronger one.'
- DELIBERATE KEEP #2 — ROOT_BLOCKS attribution with score UNCHANGED (score_build.py:56-110): six B-tier checks (payment_row_shape, total_field, chronological_order, summary_accuracy, summary_bounds_utc, resync_idempotent) are declared downstream of sync_completeness because on build 1786340680 'Tier B = 0.3611' read as twelve broken behaviours when it was ONE defect (sync returned 0 rows) seen through six windows, and sent a week of work at 'the vendor contract'. The obvious fix — marking blocked checks unscored — was tried and rejected as backwards: it would lift a totally broken app from 0.7226 to 0.8309 ('absent input must score zero, never full marks' applies in both directions). Attribution is purely additive: no score/weight/detail changes, SCORER_VERSION deliberately NOT bumped. local_pagination is deliberately EXCLUDED from the blocked set because it scores 0.33 not 0.00 on the same cells (default/cap/offset semantics remain partly checkable against an empty collection) — 'a partially-independent check does not belong in a blocked set'. attribute_root_causes only attributes a dependent when BOTH it and the root scored exactly 0; a dependent failing while the root passed keeps its own name. format_report prints the warning line ('this is ONE defect, not N+1') described as 'THE LINE THAT STOPS A TIER MEAN BEING READ AS BREADTH'.
- DELIBERATE KEEP #3 — the `parts` field in g() (score_build.py:208-225): per-item breakdown behind the aggregate, added because modules_present computed which files exist then discarded the breakdown, making file-level questions ('does a task_split child build its files worse?') untestable — the only substitute was a run-level score comparison which answers a different question 'and reversed under a corrected join when it was tried'. PURELY ADDITIVE; SCORER_VERSION deliberately NOT bumped because a bump would force re-scoring the entire archived corpus to acquire a field that changes no number.
- DELIBERATE KEEP #4 — two vacuous-truth fixes: summary_bounds_utc (score_build.py:362-364) — `all()` over an empty generator is vacuously true, so absent bounds used to earn the 0.3 UTC credit ('30% for producing nothing'); both values must now exist before either sub-score applies. ui_offline (score_build.py:399-401) — 'no external assets' is vacuously true of no page at all; an absent frontend must score zero, 'the same inverted-logic trap as the empty-bounds credit'.
- DELIBERATE REMOVAL — error_detail_quality (comment at score_build.py:546-549): it scored a 404 body of {"error": "not_found"} at 60% and wanted extra keys, but that body is EXACTLY what spec-build.md specifies. 'A check that penalises precise compliance with the stated contract is a preference dressed as a defect, and fails the traceability test in the legitimacy rule. Deleted rather than rebased: there is no defect here to measure.'
- PROBE-ERROR HANDLING: evaluate() in both score_build.py (654-656) and vendor_trace.py (230-233) wraps every check in try/except; an exception scores 0 with detail 'PROBE ERROR: …' and consequence 'probe bug' — vendor_trace comment: 'a broken probe must never look like a model failure'. format_report suppresses consequence lines for consequences in ('n/a', 'probe bug', '', None).
- VENDOR_TRACE PHASE GATING (probes/vendor_trace.py:20-33): exercise_phase grades only requests AFTER the first __phase__ marker — everything before is the agent building/testing itself, and 'grading that would score throwaway scratch code'. ANY marker opens the window: the earlier version matched only the literal 'exercise', which made the build flow's sync1/sync2 markers invisible and graded the whole trace including the agent's dev testing. With no marker at all (older traces) it grades everything 'rather than nothing'. Exception: check_read_the_docs deliberately scans the full trace including the build phase.
- VENDOR_TRACE has 14 registered checks but score_build wires only 7 as Tier C. The 7 NOT used by score_build (they need a `results` dict which score_build passes as {}): short_page_not_end (page 1 is deliberately short at 5 rows while page 2 follows), all_payments_returned (fetch_all_payments returns 47), total_semantics (total_count()==47), chronological_order (client-side ID order vs _true_order; distinguishes 'string-sorted' from chronological), idempotency_key_sent (all POSTs carry Idempotency-Key), replay_is_success (create_first == create_replay, the documented 409 path), amounts_integer_minor (no float money). vendor_trace.evaluate() scores these pass/total — it is a separate flat binary scoresheet with its own format_report.
- GATHER MECHANICS (score_build.py:569-647): boots `python -m vendorsync --db … --port …` with MERIDIAN_BASE_URL pointed at the mock vendor and MERIDIAN_API_KEY=sk_test_meridian; 25s boot deadline polling /api/health; falls back to a nested directory containing vendorsync/ if the root lacks one; issues sync twice with phase markers (sync1/sync2), then the pagination/validation/summary GETs, then TWO overlapping threaded POST /api/sync calls for concurrent_sync_safe; kills the whole process group at the end; parses the vendor trace JSONL and computes sync1_reqs/sync2_reqs/sync2_304/max_limit_used via _trace_split (which splits the trace on the phase markers and counts only GET /v1/payments entries).
- CONTROLS (controls.py): docstring names three grader properties — HIGH (known-good scores high), LOW (broken build scores low), ISOLATION (each injected defect fails ONLY its own check) — and states isolation 'is the one that matters most and is almost never checked', citing two measured incidents: a missing subcommand took a 43/45 build to 0/44, and a stale trace field scored a correct paginator 0% ('Both looked like devastating model failures and were neither'). The script actually runs two controls: known-good (default tree runs/build/opus-5-r0, copied without *.db/verdict.json/process.json/__pycache__/.swarm) must score >= 0.85; then 4 defects injected into COPIES of that tree, each must break its expected checks and nothing new (anything else is a CASCADE → FAIL). Verdict 'GRADER TRUSTED' only if both pass; written to runs/controls/controls.json.
- CONTROL DEFECTS and their design lessons (controls.py:48-83): no_frontend (rm web/) expects exactly {modules_present, serves_page, ui_states, ui_currency, ui_offline, ui_polish, ui_error_actionable}; declared_iface_incomplete renames total_count — chosen because the app never calls it at runtime; the first version renamed last_sync, which IS called on the health path, crashed the server and cascaded 24 checks — 'a badly designed control, not a grader fault: a defect that breaks the app at runtime SHOULD break everything downstream'; default_limit_wrong (25→7) expects only local_pagination; upsert_not_atomic converts INSERT INTO payments to INSERT OR REPLACE and strips the ON CONFLICT clause, keeping the write CORRECT but non-atomic so only store_atomic_upsert moves — 'Deleting ON CONFLICT outright broke the SQL and nothing persisted at all', and renaming the table 'broke persistence entirely and cascaded, which tested nothing about the atomicity check'.
- SATURATABLE BY CONSTRUCTION (easy full marks): all 6 Tier A checks (file existence, AST method names, boot, key presence — the floor by design); in B: total_field, ui_states/ui_currency/ui_offline (regex-over-HTML — the right words/function names in the page saturate them), input_validation and local_pagination (three concrete behaviours each), payment_row_shape (exact 5 keys), sync_completeness/resync_idempotent (saturate at correct behaviour); ALL 7 C-tier checks are binary — a client that handles the trap chain once gets 7×1.0 with zero headroom; in D: api_content_type, store_indexed, client_timeouts, ui_error_actionable, store_atomic_upsert, ui_polish are regex/binary source-or-HTML greps (writing 'ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE', 'timeout=', a <th>, and the word 'retry' saturates them), uses_max_limit saturates by simply requesting limit=100, concurrent_sync_safe is binary. That is ~26 of 35 checks saturatable. The ONLY checks with structural headroom against an optimum: request_efficiency (exactly 7 vendor requests required for 1.0; paging at default 25 is 'correct but not efficient') and resync_conditional_ratio (1.0 requires EVERY second-sync request answered 304). Both are still exactly reachable by an optimal client, so despite the docstring, no check is mathematically un-saturatable — Tier D's 'deliberately hard to max' rests on just those two ratio checks, and 5 of 10 D checks are plain binary.

### Mature code-gen benchmarks fight ceiling effects with seven recurring mechanisms: (1) harder instance selection by construction — SWE-bench Pro excludes 1-10 LOC edits and requires multi-file ~107-LOC solutions, dropping frontier scores from >70% to ~23%; (2) contamination-resistant rotation — LiveCodeBench's rolling training-cutoff windows, SWE-bench-Live's +50 verified issues/month, SWE-rebench's fresh post-cutoff issues; (3) private held-out splits — SWE-bench Pro's 858-problem held-out set and 276 commercial (purchased startup) problems, SWE-bench Multimodal's private 517-instance test split graded only via cloud sb-cli, plus GPL/copyleft repo selection as a legal contamination barrier; (4) test amplification on unchanged instances — EvalPlus/HumanEval+ coverage-guided type-aware mutation fuzzing (80x tests, 10-20pt pass@1 drops) and BigCodeBench's 99%-branch-coverage suites; (5) partial-credit / rubric scoring — WebGen-Bench's (Yes + 0.5*Partial)/Total, E2EDev's two-granularity BDD scoring (requirement-level vs 703 individual scenario-level), ProjDevBench's weighted test points with OJ verdict granularity plus a 20%-weight LLM code review; (6) weighted tiers — SWE-Lancer's real dollar payouts ($50-$32,000 per task, $1M total) as continuous weights and LiveCodeBench Pro's Elo tiers (hard >3000 where frontier models score ~0%); (7) orthogonal axes past correctness — efficiency metrics (ENAMEL eff@k, Mercury Beyond@K) and vision-LLM appearance scores 0-5. All mechanisms with citations are in the facts list.

- HARDER INSTANCE SELECTION (SWE-bench Pro, Scale AI, arXiv 2509.16941): explicitly excludes trivial edits of 1-10 LOC; every problem requires >=10 changed lines and 100+ tasks require >100 lines; reference solutions average 107.4 LOC across 4.1 files (median 55.0) vs SWE-bench Verified's mean 11.6 LOC / median 4.5 — the paper notes 161 of Verified's 500 instances need only 1-2 line edits. Effect: frontier models >70% on Verified fall to <=23.3% (public set) / <=17.8% (commercial set). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941
- HELD-OUT + LICENSE-BASED CONTAMINATION RESISTANCE (SWE-bench Pro): 1,865 problems split into a public set (731 instances, 11 repos, all GPL/copyleft — copyleft chosen because it creates legal barriers to inclusion in commercial training corpora), a private held-out set (858 problems, 12 separate repos, kept private explicitly 'to test for overfitting in the future' against the public set), and a commercial set (276 problems, 18 proprietary startup codebases purchased by Scale). Each repo capped at ~100 instances to prevent single-repo overfitting. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941 and https://static.scale.com/uploads/654197dc94d34f66c0f5184e/SWEAP_Eval_Scale%20(9).pdf
- FALSE-NEGATIVE REMOVAL VIA HUMAN AUGMENTATION (SWE-bench Pro): each task ships a human-augmented problem statement plus an explicit 'Requirements' list grounded on the validation unit tests and an explicit 'Interface' section naming expected classes/functions (kills the misnamed-symbol false-negative mode). Tests are split fail2pass (verify fix) / pass2pass (no regressions), each suite is run 3 times to filter flaky tests, and annotators additionally filter fail2pass tests that are 'too broad or not relevant'. https://static.scale.com/uploads/654197dc94d34f66c0f5184e/SWEAP_Eval_Scale%20(9).pdf
- HUMAN-VALIDATED SUBSET AS NOISE FLOOR FIX (SWE-bench Verified, OpenAI 2024): 93 professional developers screened the original SWE-bench test set down to 500 samples, removing incorrect grading of correct solutions, under-specified issue statements, and overly-specific unit tests; OpenAI also released per-instance difficulty annotations for ALL SWE-bench test samples, enabling difficulty-sliced (harder-subset) evaluation. https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/
- PRIVATE TEST SPLIT + CLOUD-ONLY GRADING (SWE-bench Multimodal, arXiv 2410.03859): 619 JavaScript task instances from 17 visual-domain repos (data viz, maps, UI components); the 517-instance test split is kept private and leaderboard submissions run through the sb-cli cloud evaluation tool — grading without releasing the answers. 90.6% of test instances contain images; human annotation found visual input required for 83.5% of issues. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03859
- MONTHLY INSTANCE ROTATION (SWE-bench-Live, Microsoft, NeurIPS 2025 D&B): automated curation pipeline adds 50 newly verified GitHub issues per month; 1,890 tasks from 223 repositories, all post-2024 issues, each with a dedicated Docker image — contamination resistance by continuous refresh rather than secrecy. https://github.com/microsoft/SWE-bench-Live
- ROLLING TRAINING-CUTOFF WINDOWS (LiveCodeBench, arXiv 2403.07974): every problem is timestamped from its contest release date (LeetCode/AtCoder/Codeforces); a model is only scored on problems released AFTER its training cutoff, making contamination impossible by construction; problems carry EASY/MEDIUM/HARD difficulty labels for granular comparison, and problems rated too difficult for any current model are excluded to keep the metric informative. https://livecodebench.github.io/
- EXPERT-CALIBRATED DIFFICULTY TIERS THAT PRESERVE HEADROOM (LiveCodeBench Pro, arXiv 2506.11928): 584 continuously-updated Codeforces/ICPC/IOI problems tiered by Codeforces Elo — easy <=2000, medium 2000-3000, hard >3000; Olympiad medalists annotate every problem (skill tags; knowledge/observation/logic-heavy) via triple-blind adjudication and do line-by-line failure analysis of model submissions; frontier models score ~0% on the hard tier, deliberately keeping the top of the scale open. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11928
- TEST AMPLIFICATION ON UNCHANGED INSTANCES (HumanEval+ / EvalPlus): original HumanEval averaged ~7 tests/problem; EvalPlus instruments the reference solution for statement/branch coverage and generates ~80x more tests via LLM-seeded, type-aware mutation fuzzing (flip booleans, perturb strings, add/remove list elements). Same 164 problems, stricter oracle: pass@1 drops 10-20 points across models (GPT-4: 88.4% HumanEval -> 76.2% HumanEval+), restoring separation without new tasks. https://evalplus.github.io/leaderboard.html and https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/humaneval-184d0fb5-b481-4681-aca4-8f5a7f000fca
- HIGH-COVERAGE SUITES + COMPOSITIONAL TASKS + SPEC VARIANT (BigCodeBench, arXiv 2406.15877): 1,140 tasks requiring composition of function calls from 139 libraries across 7 domains; each task has on average 5.6 test cases with 99% average branch coverage; a parallel BigCodeBench-Instruct variant auto-transforms the docstring spec into a terse NL instruction — two spec renderings of the same task set (multi-spec evaluation). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15877
- ECONOMIC VALUE AS CONTINUOUS WEIGHT (SWE-Lancer, OpenAI, arXiv 2502.12115): 1,400+ real Upwork freelance tasks worth $1M total in actual historical payouts; score is dollars earned, so each task is weighted by its market price ($50 bug fixes to $32,000 features) — a naturally graded, non-uniform tier system. IC SWE tasks (764, $414K) graded by end-to-end tests triple-verified by engineers; SWE Manager tasks (724, $585K) graded against the real hiring manager's chosen proposal. Best model at release earned ~$208K of $500.8K on the Diamond set. https://openai.com/index/swe-lancer/
- PARTIAL-CREDIT UI-AGENT GRADING + APPEARANCE RUBRIC (WebGen-Bench, arXiv 2505.03733): each generated website is exercised by a UI agent (WebVoyager) running per-task test operations; outcomes classified Yes / Partial / No / Start-Failed, scored Accuracy = (N_Yes + 0.5 x N_Partial) / N_Total; separately, a vision-LLM grades each rendered site 0-5 on layout/rendering/modernness (0 = blank/broken, 5 = polished). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03733
- TWO-GRANULARITY BDD RUBRIC (E2EDev, arXiv 2510.14509): 46 app-building projects decomposed into 244 fine-grained user requirements and 703 Gherkin BDD scenarios with human-verified Python Behave step implementations (avg 5.3 requirements / 15.3 tests per project); scoring at two granularities — Requirement Accuracy (requirement counts only if ALL its scenarios pass), Test Accuracy (individual scenario pass rate), plus a Soft Requirement Accuracy for partial fulfillment and a balanced score correcting for uneven tests-per-requirement. Built via HITL-MAA multi-agent annotation with human review (~20% of requirements and ~50% of test cases needed human adjustment). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14509
- WEIGHTED TEST POINTS + VERDICT GRANULARITY + LLM CODE REVIEW (ProjDevBench, arXiv 2602.01655): end-to-end project development graded on an Online-Judge platform with weighted test cases (S_raw = sum of w_i x 1[test i passed], normalized 0-100; harder test points can carry higher weights) and fine-grained verdicts (compile error / runtime error / TLE / MLE / wrong answer) instead of binary pass/fail; final score = 0.8 x execution + 0.2 x LLM-based code review that catches forbidden-library use and cheating solutions that exploit test weaknesses (LLM review accuracy 0.852, Cohen's kappa 0.710 vs experts); max score across allowed submissions is reported. https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01655
- TYPED REWARD FUNCTIONS FOR OPEN-ENDED TASKS (WebArena, arXiv 2307.13854): functional-correctness grading via four evidence types per task — exact_match (standardized answers), must_include (key-concept containment), LLM fuzzy_match (semantic equivalence, GPT-4-judged), and programmatic validation of final site/DB state via APIs, database queries, or DOM/JS selectors — grading the outcome state, not the trajectory. VisualWebArena and WebChoreArena extend the same scheme. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13854
- EFFICIENCY AS A POST-CORRECTNESS AXIS: ENAMEL defines eff@k, generalizing pass@k from correctness to runtime efficiency on curated HumanEval/MBPP subsets; Mercury (1,889 tasks) ships per-task unlimited test-case GENERATORS and a Beyond@K metric normalizing runtime against the historical LeetCode submission distribution; EffiBench (1,000 problems) scores against SOTA-efficiency canonical solutions — GPT-4 code averaged 3.12x canonical runtime (up to 13.89x, memory up to 43.92x). Correct-but-slow separates models that tie on pass rate. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15dc4534ae1e5aefdc196cf1da983b2ee56c1dda and https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d4032b47c1e5b9c51df5e3c4e33fea72761ff5da
- SATURATION STATE + 2026 SUCCESSOR WAVE (AgentMarketCap, Apr 2026): by early 2026 five models from four labs sat statistically tied near 80% on SWE-bench Verified (Opus 4.5/4.6 ~80.9%, Gemini 3.1 Pro 80.6%, GPT-5.2 80.0%) — the benchmark 'stopped discriminating at the frontier'. Documented responses beyond SWE-bench Pro: SWE-EVO (long-horizon software evolution, avg 21 files touched per problem), FeatureBench (200 hard tasks from 24 repos exposing gaps invisible on Verified), Terminal-Bench (sandboxed multi-step CLI workflows), and SWE-rebench (re-evaluates all models with ONE standardized scaffold on fresh post-cutoff issues, removing scaffold confounds). https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/10/swe-bench-saturation-90-percent-coding-benchmarks
- MECHANISM TAXONOMY (cross-benchmark synthesis of the above): (1) harder-instance filters with hard LOC/file minimums (SWE-bench Pro); (2) temporal rotation — rolling cutoffs (LiveCodeBench) or monthly refresh (SWE-bench-Live, SWE-rebench); (3) held-out/private splits + copyleft or purchased-private code as legal contamination barriers (SWE-bench Pro, SWE-bench Multimodal); (4) test amplification on existing instances via coverage-guided fuzzing (EvalPlus) or near-total branch coverage (BigCodeBench); (5) partial credit — 0.5-weight partials (WebGen-Bench), per-scenario vs per-requirement granularity (E2EDev), weighted test points with OJ verdicts (ProjDevBench); (6) weighted tiers — dollar values (SWE-Lancer), Elo difficulty tiers (LiveCodeBench Pro), difficulty annotations for subset slicing (SWE-bench Verified); (7) orthogonal scoring axes — efficiency (eff@k, Beyond@K), visual quality 0-5 (WebGen-Bench), LLM code-review compliance (ProjDevBench); (8) multi-spec rendering of the same tasks (BigCodeBench-Instruct; E2EDev requirements-vs-no-requirements setting in SWE-bench Pro).
