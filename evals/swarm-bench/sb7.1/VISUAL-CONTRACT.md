# SB7.1 — payment towers and committed-update inspection

This extends the supplied SB7 contract. Preserve its full payment collection, money arithmetic, Berlin-day placement, stable arrival indices, transaction semantics, fault recovery, raw WebGL, GPU picking, camera interaction, linked brush, label culling, SSE diffs, and rendering budgets. There is no new backend service. Only the geometry and the explicitly described inspection mode below supersede the old solid-column shape and fixed camera target.

## Overview: a payment is a structured tower

Keep the original center `(x,z)`, original height `h`, base `y=0`, and maximum footprint `0.9 × 0.9`. Render each payment as the union of these four centered, axis-aligned closed solids; dimensions are world units and vertical fractions multiply that payment's `h`:

| Part | Width and depth | Vertical interval |
|---|---|---|
| Pedestal | 0.90 | 0 to 0.12h |
| Shaft | 0.54 | 0.12h to 0.90h |
| Status cap | 0.90 | 0.90h to h |
| Currency collar | EUR 0.62; USD 0.70; JPY 0.78; KWD 0.86 | 0.76h to 0.84h |

The collar overlaps the shaft. Surface intersection and occlusion are the union of the actual solids, not the enclosing rectangular box. Both visible rendering and the offscreen identity pass use that same structure. A ray through the open shoulder beside the narrow shaft must not select an invisible bounding-box face.

Keep the exact SB7 status colors and dimming rules. Only the absolute top surface `y=h` uses full status color. For explicit directional depth, X-normal vertical surfaces use factor 0.55, Z-normal vertical surfaces use 0.72, and all other horizontal surfaces use 0.82. Multiply the status RGB by that factor and round each channel; apply the brush dim before the factor. This published directional shading supersedes SB7’s uniform side factor. No gradients, textures or outline replace this diagnostic color encoding. No amount, height, position, or status may be invented for visual effect.

The original data digest still represents one payment and one `h`, not four parts. The four parts must fit within the existing draw and upload budgets; a scene assembled from four unbatched draws per payment does not pass. The expanded pick oracle uses the four-solid union, while the original field layout, identity, depth, moment, height, and streaming requirements remain.

## Detailed inspection: the ledger spire

The overview above remains unchanged. Inspection renders a detailed version of the **same real payment** on the same canvas. This is an explicit inspection-only exception to SB7's prohibition on LOD: every overview payment still uses all four published solids, and no payment is omitted or decimated. The detailed object retains its exact center, amount-derived height, maximum 0.90 footprint and status truth.

In inspection, replace the overview solids with this union of closed solids. Coordinates below are relative to the payment center in X/Z, while Y fractions multiply its actual height:

- Pedestal: y=0..0.12h; status cap: y=0.90h..h. Both have an octagonal footprint defined by `abs(x)<=0.45`, `abs(z)<=0.45`, `abs(x)+abs(z)<=0.80`. These are clipped vertical corners, not rounded texture details.
- Recessed core shaft: width/depth 0.38, y=0.12h..0.90h.
- Four separate support ribs: width/depth 0.06, centered at each combination of x=±0.24, z=±0.24, y=0.12h..0.90h. They are actual geometry, not painted stripes.
- Hollow currency frame: outer width/depth remains EUR 0.62, USD 0.70, JPY 0.78, KWD 0.86. Four rails of thickness 0.04 enclose a square opening; y=0.76h..0.84h at rest. The EUR frame's inner corners may touch the ribs' outer corners. The opening is not filled; core/ribs/background seen through it must survive depth testing and picking.

Use a deliberate material hierarchy: core RGB (42,55,73), pedestal/ribs (190,207,223), status cap the original status RGB, and currency frame EUR (37,99,235), USD (6,182,212), JPY (234,88,12), KWD (147,51,234). Apply factor 0.55 to X-normal faces, 0.72 to Z-normal faces, and 0.82 to horizontal faces except the cap's absolute top, which uses 1. For a clipped vertical corner with normal `(nx,0,nz)`, factor is `(0.55*abs(nx)+0.72*abs(nz))/(abs(nx)+abs(nz))`. Round each resulting RGB channel. Inspection is undimmed. This gives dimensional contrast and distinct structural materials without adding a lighting library, textures or another service.

Both visible and identity passes use this actual detailed union while inspecting. The frame follows the committed-update trajectory below as one rigid hollow structure; the core, ribs, pedestal and cap remain stationary. Its movement must expose the actual structure behind it. Layout, typography, control styling and presentation are authored by the application; do not copy a separate showcase. The field and inspector should feel like one finished payments product.

## Animate an actual committed payment update

When the currently inspected payment receives a newer committed SSE version, immediately apply its authoritative data as required by SB7 and animate its currency collar. This is a **payment update**, not a claim that money moved or a payment settled. Amount, height, pedestal, shaft, ribs, cap and status truth remain fixed by the backend throughout the animation.

Let `t` be elapsed seconds since receiving that newer version, clamped to `[0,1]`, and `s=t²(3−2t)`. For exactly one second the collar's bottom is `(0.20 + 0.56s)h` and its top is `(0.28 + 0.56s)h`. Its width remains currency-dependent. At the end it rests in its original interval `[0.76h,0.84h]` and demand rendering resumes. The camera does not move as a substitute for object animation. A newer update of that inspected payment restarts this trajectory from the new version. Do not animate duplicate or stale versions. Leaving inspection cancels its presentation animation without changing any backend state.

Keep the latest actually observed committed update for each payment while the page is open. Replay payment update is disabled until the inspected payment has such an update; clicking it replays that same one-second presentation without issuing a write or changing payment truth. `#replay-status` states **Payment update**, the version, and whether this is live or replay. Reloaded pages need not retain presentation replay history; backend durability remains governed by SB7.

## Evidence and grading

The SB7.1 probe retains the full SB7 checks. Its additional structural checks compare the actual visible framebuffer and GPU picks against independently projected detailed geometry for one seeded payment from every currency at inspector yaw 35 and 125 degrees. Positive pedestal/core/cap/rib/frame/chamfer surfaces and negative cut-corner/open-frame witnesses each have separate coverage and match requirements; broad background cannot hide a filled gap. At least three stable samples per feature are required at the published desktop inspector size; unavailable analytic coverage is a harness error, not a candidate failure. Color tolerance remains ±8/channel; rays use the centers of the actual sampled framebuffer pixels, and edge samples are excluded where any of the eight neighboring pixels changes analytic surface. The legacy blind 24-point context grid remains diagnostic: the context-presence check uses independently predicted seeded surface pixels so a taller canvas and narrower shafts cannot fail solely because a coarse grid misses them. The reference must demonstrate that these comparisons separate missing-part, solid-box, wrong-collar-width, and fabricated/offscreen-only renderers. The overview still uses the original four-solid union for all inherited field tests.

The inherited stream-application latency requirement includes visible pixels, not only a debug digest. For the scheduled D1 status mutation, the scorer waits until the probe has placed and brushed the seeded target at an independently verified close-up surface. It then delivers the original real vendor mutation exactly once. The browser stamps receipt before application handlers and records the first completed default-framebuffer draw whose 3 × 3 target pixels match the actual new wire status, directional shading and required post-update brush state. The pre-update pixels must differ, and the camera remains stationary during this measurement. A fast CPU/debug update followed by a delayed GPU update earns the measured delayed latency; no correct pixel application means no latency credit. Readback overhead remains in the measurement rather than being subtracted. This replaces the old geometry-only digest-change detector, which cannot observe a legitimate status-only update. No additional candidate debug API is required.

Visible-surface admission is measured in the same browser/context/state as its screenshot: a visible and substantially unobscured canvas must show pixels matching at least eight independently selected seeded payment identities, not merely arbitrary colored pixels or a working debug object. Missing, covered, blank, fallback-only, and offscreen-only surfaces do not receive admission. Partial fine-grained correctness remains visible in the report; a hidden defining visualization is a root failure.

For live-animation evidence, the probe inspects a real payment, makes one legitimate note edit through the existing public API, observes its actual SSE version, and corroborates the resulting version/note through the payment API. It schedules ten intermediate and one settled framebuffer samples, recording both read time and the independently instrumented last default-framebuffer draw time of this exact visible canvas, plus independently composited browser screenshots during motion and at rest (the compositor instant is bounded by the measured screenshot request/response interval) against the printed trajectory and a stationary-camera control, then uses the real replay button and verifies backend state did not change. Child text is checked for its own contrast, clipping, exposure and actual screenshot ink; a readable parent container does not prove its descendants readable. Two further legitimate note commits test whether a newer version restarts the trajectory. The probe re-delivers actual previously observed records through the existing EventSource to test duplicate/stale suppression, then exits inspection during replay and verifies the default camera and demand-rendering idle state return, with no remaining rendered annotations (including after scrolling). Emptying annotation content and CSS hiding are both valid; an HTML `hidden` attribute is not required. Re-delivery is labeled test transport, not a new backend commit. A static collar, stepped approximation, decorative autonomous loop, fabricated event, or camera-only animation does not satisfy this test. Pixel trajectory comparison uses one consistent animation clock across the complete live/replay/restart battery: either the independently intercepted RAF callback timestamp for a draw inside that callback, or the actual monotonic draw timestamp. Outside RAF it uses the actual draw timestamp. The probe preserves both clock-model results and chooses one complete-battery model, never a different clock per pixel or frame. The callback timestamp is recorded with try/finally and cannot be supplied through candidate debug data. Screenshot matching uses actual recorded draws during its request interval, including the last frame before the request, rather than assuming the frame timestamp equals the screenshot read time. Comparison and excludes positions whose predicted surface changes within ±16 ms or the neighboring raster pixels. Each eligible moving sample must show both the expected general geometry and positively occupied moving-collar pixels; an empty former collar position alone is not motion evidence. At least nine eligible framebuffer samples are required, including at least one correct occupied-frame witness in each elapsed interval [0,300), [300,600), and [600,900) ms under the one chosen clock. Repeated reads of a single held intermediate frame cannot substitute for that phase coverage. This is not a fixed-FPS requirement; actual read times, draw times, repeated frames, frame age and capture intervals remain in the report. The finite animation duration is a product behavior, not a limit on the model's implementation work.

These deterministic requirements measure structural correctness, readable dimensional presentation and interaction quality. They do not turn a perfect score into a claim of subjective artistic excellence. The report keeps named checks, expected/observed measurements, screenshots and a video excerpt from this same graded browser recording. No separate showcase app, replacement scene, or post-grading reenactment substitutes for evidence. Report missing media explicitly. The full existing SB7 tier breakdown and critical consequences remain available alongside these added structural, presentation and motion checks.
